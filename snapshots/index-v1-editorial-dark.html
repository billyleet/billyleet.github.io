LANGUAGE

01   About

Language was always the obsession. The medium just changed.

Before UX, I was a Speech-Language Pathologist. I spent years sitting across from people who knew exactly what they wanted to say but couldn't get the words to cooperate, and I became fascinated by that gap between intent and expression. That's still the problem I'm working on. The medium just changed from mouths to screens.

The obsession started even earlier than that. In 7th grade, I taught myself the Elvish writing system from Tolkien's appendices so I could pass coded notes to friends in class. When something is interesting enough, you figure out how to use it. Language has always been interesting enough.

I've spent over seven years doing UX Research at Instagram, Meta, and Duolingo. At all three, understanding how people communicate was central to the work. I've led generative studies that shifted roadmap priorities, usability research that caught critical failures before they shipped, and longitudinal work that traced how people's relationships with a product evolve over months.

I'm currently available for senior contract and full-time UX Research roles. If you're building something where understanding how people communicate is the job, I'd like to talk.

02   What I Do

01

Research Strategy

The best research projects start with a precise question, not a vague one. I get involved before the brief is written to help teams figure out exactly what they need to learn.

02

Qualitative Research

Interviews, ethnographic observation, diary studies, usability testing. I'm skilled at creating conditions where people tell you what they actually think, not what they think you want to hear.

03

Mixed Methods

Qualitative findings gain force when triangulated with behavioral data. I'm comfortable partnering with data scientists and designing surveys that complement exploratory work.

04

Synthesis & Storytelling

After fieldwork, I put real time into synthesis: what the patterns mean, how to sequence the story, and what format will reach the people who need to act on it.

05

Communication & Language

I trained as a Speech-Language Pathologist before moving into UX research. That background gives me a specific vocabulary for diagnosing where communication breaks down, which changes the questions I know to ask.

06

Stakeholder Influence

Getting research into decisions is a separate skill from doing the research. I pay close attention to who makes decisions, what they need to hear, and when in the process they're actually open to changing course.
